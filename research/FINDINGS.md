## 1. Ride-through models and curtailment proof for NERC-registered data centers

`datacenter-as-grid-entity-compliance` · **Physical / industrial** · 🔴 **Recall-only** — no live crowding search; competitor claims are model recall

> Boutique that builds the filed dynamic model (EPRI UDM/CMLD), runs disturbance-response testing on UPS/rectifier/chiller settings, and assembles the NERC attestation package per campus at $150k-$600k, plus the signed metering record proving each ERCOT curtailment.

**Score 6.28/10** — overlooked 5 · inflection 8 · buildable 7 · market 6 · defensibility 5

**Why now**

Four dated triggers have already fired. NERC issued a Level 3 Essential Actions Alert on 4 May 2026 — only the third in its 58-year history — with entity responses due 3 August 2026. FERC issued an order in Docket RD26-7-000 on 16 July 2026 directing NERC to file mandatory reliability standards for computational loads by 31 December 2026 and a Phase II work plan by 1 March 2027. FERC issued tailored Section 206 orders to every RTO on 18 June 2026 (Docket RM26-4) to accelerate large-load interconnection. ERCOT's NOGRR 282 grandfathers only facilities energized or study-complete on or before 14 November 2025 and imposes a 90-day root-cause / 90-day corrective-plan / 180-day implementation cycle after any failed ride-through. ERCOT is tracking more than 438 GW of large-load requests with roughly 198 GW applying in Q1 2026 alone, nearly 90% data centers — every one of which will need a filed, defensible model and a performance record by 2029-2031.

**Why it is overlooked**

The entire 'data center power' startup wave aimed at generation, siting and physical flexing (Emerald AI, VoltaGrid, Verrus, Build.inc), and the adjacent software money went to generator interconnection queue automation (Google/Tapestry HyperQ with PJM, Enverus SUGAR with MISO). Neither touches the load-side reliability-compliance obligation or the paperwork-and-proof layer that decides whether a curtailable interconnection agreement is granted, honored and paid for. Today that work sits with a handful of tiny consultancies (EPE Consulting, Keentel Engineering) and hyperscaler internal EE teams that have never filed a NERC-facing dynamic model; large-load interconnection is so new as a formal construct that incumbent transmission-planning software does not model it and consulting engineers do it by hand. NERC's own Large Loads Task Force white papers (Jul 2025, Mar 2026) describe this as a gap in existing practices and Reliability Standards.

**The 90-day wedge**

Two power-systems engineers with PSS/E and PSCAD licenses and EPRI UDM familiarity, starting in ERCOT where the construct is furthest along. 90 days: publish a free 'NOGRR 282 / NERC Level 3 Alert readiness gap assessment' aimed specifically at ERCOT large loads that are NOT grandfathered (energized after 14 November 2025), then convert to the paid dynamic model build plus UPS transfer-threshold study — field data collects off existing PQ meters at the point of interconnection, and the scarce input is the person who can translate IEC 62040-3 UPS transfer behavior into an ISO-acceptable load model. Second product, sold to the same three data center developers already paying consultancies per filing: the PCLR/Batch Zero registration and modeling data pack (PGRR145 artifacts, ride-through documentation, self-limiting control descriptions) as guided software, plus a metering pipeline that emits a signed, timestamped curtailment performance record against each dispatch instruction.

**Why this rank**

Best risk-adjusted bet in the pool: four dated triggers have ALREADY fired (NERC Level 3 Alert 4 May 2026 with responses due 3 Aug 2026 — literally this week; FERC RD26-7 ordering mandatory computational-load standards by 31 Dec 2026; RM26-4 §206 orders to every RTO 18 Jun 2026), so revenue exists in month one rather than after a rule lands. No license gate, no capital, no enterprise sales cycle — the buyer is under an enforcement clock. Two or three power-systems engineers can start Monday. Re-validation after every trip event makes it recurring, and ERCOT PCLR registration is a second product on the same relationship. Overlooked only scored 5 because Certrec, 1898 & Co and Burns & McDonnell can pivot — but those firms are capacity-constrained and the specific artifact (validated EMT models for power-electronic compute load) is new to everyone, so the head start is real. Ranked above nuclear and customs because it needs no exam, no ACE certification, and no three-year audit history before the first invoice.

## 2. Entry Type 13 and instrument-aware duty recovery for the post-de-minimis long tail

`long-tail-customs-entry-and-duty-recovery` · **Regulatory clock** · 🔴 **Recall-only** — no live crowding search; competitor claims are model recall

> Software letting one licensed broker supervise 50-100x entry throughput on low-value shipments, sold as entry-as-a-service to SMB importers, bolted to instrument-aware drawback recovery (Section 301 eligible, Section 232 generally not).

**Score 6.24/10** — overlooked 4 · inflection 8 · buildable 6 · market 8 · defensibility 6

**Why now**

De minimis ended for China/Hong Kong on 2 May 2025 and for all countries on 29 August 2025; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed 4 July 2025 permanently repeals the statutory basis effective 1 July 2027; and on 24 June 2026 CBP published an indefinite suspension of the administrative exemption. FY2024 saw over 1.36 billion de minimis packages enter the US. The integration door opens in seven weeks: CBP begins a voluntary ACE test of Entry Type 13 — a fully electronic informal-entry path for shipments valued at $2,500 or less — on 22 September 2026. On the recovery side, search-level sources report that following a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling (6-3) that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, CBP launched the CAPE refund system on 20 April 2026, with roughly 75,300 CAPE declarations covering over 11.2 million entries submitted by 26 April — six days of filings covering eleven million entries. VERIFY BEFORE ACTING: the scout could not page-verify the SCOTUS holding or the CAPE figures; treat them as strong search extracts, not diligence. The strategic read is unchanged either way — striking IEEPA channels protectionism into Section 232/301/122 instruments with different eligibility and refund mechanics, creating a permanent reclassification and recovery surface.

**Why it is overlooked**

The visible startups — Zonos, Passport, Portless, Flexport — sell cross-border to consumer brands with real volume and real ops teams. The stranded population is the small domestic importer: the Etsy-scale seller, the parts distributor, the specialty retailer bringing in a pallet, whom 3PLs and forwarders are actively turning away. Nobody has attacked the broker-capacity bottleneck itself, which is the actual scarce asset, because investors read 'customs brokerage' as a low-margin services business. On recovery, roughly 80% of eligible refunds are never filed and about $15 billion in eligible tariff refunds go unclaimed annually — true for years because matching import entry lines to export lines cost more than it recovered below Fortune-500 scale; Charter Brokerage alone accounts for approximately one-third of all US duty drawback refunds, on 50/50 contingency splits and 9-12 month timelines. Honest caveat: 'best duty drawback companies in 2026' listicles now exist, so plain drawback-as-a-service is no longer uncontested — the unwritten layer is instrument-aware planning (drawback vs. FTZ vs. bonded warehouse vs. first-sale valuation vs. tariff engineering, per SKU per instrument), which today is sold as $800/hour trade-attorney time.

**The 90-day wedge**

90 days, two people plus one licensed customs broker as co-founder, advisor or broker-of-record — the license is a hiring problem, not a technical one. Product one: an HTS classification assistant plus an ACE ABI filing pipeline aimed at Entry Type 13, joining CBP's 22 September 2026 voluntary test as a day-one participant, delivered as a Shopify/WooCommerce/marketplace app that classifies a merchant's SKU catalog once, stores rulings and origin evidence, and auto-files at a flat per-entry price well under the incumbent $50-100. Product two, sold to the same accounts: ingest ACE entry summary data (importers can grant agent portal access today) plus export/destruction records and ship an eligibility triage that says, per entry line, which instrument applied, whether it is drawback-eligible, and the dollars at stake — priced flat, which is the obvious wedge against 50/50 contingency incumbents. Distribution: 3PLs and freight forwarders currently declining small accounts.

**Why this rank**

The only genuine compounding regulatory moat in the entire 77: a licensed profession with exam-gated supply (12-22% pass rate) meeting a legislated ~100x volume shock (1.36B FY2024 de minimis parcels), where the license, the bond and the accumulated per-SKU ruling corpus all compound and none can be cloned by a software competitor. The statutory spine is the hardest in the pool — OBBBA permanently repeals the commercial de minimis statute effective 1 Jul 2027, which no executive action reverses — and the duty-recovery half is the single most credible 'LLMs newly make this cheap' claim here because it is legal-grade reasoning over structured data with a dollar-denominated output. Ranked 2 not 1 on two real drags: ACE/ABI certification is a 6-12 month regulatory slog outside the 90-day frame, and Zonos (~$69M, already licensed and filing automated low-value entries) can move down-market faster than a new entrant acquires the tail. Entry Type 13 and the 22 Sep 2026 ACE test are unverified and load-bearing — diligence before capital.

## 3. NQA-1 qualification and commercial grade dedication as a service

`nuclear-naval-supplier-qualification` · **Physical / industrial** · 🔴 **Recall-only** — no live crowding search; competitor claims are model recall

> Fixed-fee turnkey installation of an Appendix B / NQA-1 quality system into existing AS9100 shops, plus an outsourced dedicating-entity service with a structured critical-characteristics library for commercial grade dedication.

**Score 6.48/10** — overlooked 6 · inflection 7 · buildable 8 · market 5 · defensibility 6

**Why now**

The Nuclear Scaling Initiative's landscape report, covered by ANS on 23 March 2026, names the qualification pathway itself as the bottleneck — 'strict quality standards, including NQA-1, narrow the supplier base and slow expansion,' and without 'more efficient, repeatable approval pathways' the domestic industrial base cannot grow — alongside shortfalls in nuclear-qualified machinists, welders, inspectors and NDE specialists. Demand finally broke the chicken-and-egg deadlock in 2025-2026 via hyperscaler nuclear PPAs, plant restarts, the ADVANCE Act, DOE reactor pilot programs and a federal 300 GW-by-2050 target. On the naval side the money is already obligated: a roughly $950M NAVSEA award to BlueForge Alliance and 169 supplier projects totaling $648M across 32 states, against a submarine supplier base that has contracted from 17,000 to about 5,000 since the Cold War. Qualification takes 12-36 months, so shops that start in 2026 are the ones with capacity when first-of-a-kind SMR fabrication and fleet orders land in 2028-2031 — every month of delay now is a schedule slip then.

**Why it is overlooked**

Nuclear venture capital went almost entirely to reactor developers, fuel (HALEU) and now data-center PPAs. The qualification layer is served by aging QA consultancies billing hourly with no leverage, by ASME certification (a credential, not a service), and by incumbents like Curtiss-Wright and Paragon whose model is selling their own dedicated parts rather than enabling other people's suppliers. The problem is described in the trade literature as 'workforce qualification,' which reads as a training problem and gets answered with curricula rather than software. The reuse infrastructure entrenches the gap: NUPIC eligibility requires sponsorship by five member licensees and enrolls you into a 33-month audit cycle designed for the operating LWR fleet, and NIAC is likewise an incumbent club. Searches for nuclear supplier qualification software return NRC pages, EPRI reports and consortium PDFs — no vendor. (Evidence caveat: two of the three scouts were blocked from opening pages and worked at search-extract level.)

**The 90-day wedge**

Two to three people: ideally one ex-NUPIC lead auditor, one ex-supplier quality manager, one technical writer. 90-day wedge, product one: a fixed-price 'Appendix B readiness sprint' sold to five non-nuclear precision shops in Tennessee, Ohio and the Carolinas that already hold AS9100 or ISO 9001 and are chasing SMR RFQs — deliver the QA manual, procedure set and a mock audit for roughly $75-150k per shop plus an ongoing audit retainer. Product two, higher margin and faster to cash: commercial grade dedication on named obsolete or long-lead items for operating plants, which wins on both cost and schedule against reverse engineering or plant modification. Do not try to replace NUPIC — build the CGD workbench (critical-characteristics library keyed to common component families, guided Method 1-4 workflow, exportable dedication package) and sell it to two advanced-reactor developers and their top ten suppliers, where identical parts are being dedicated repeatedly across buyers and the reuse value is immediately measurable. Referral channel: reactor developers and BlueForge-funded supplier expansion programs with a starved supply base.

**Why this rank**

Highest round-1 composite (6.48) and the bottleneck is correctly identified: qualification, not capital or reactor design, gates both the nuclear build-out and the submarine industrial base, and NUPIC is a closed utility club new entrants structurally cannot join. Demand broke the chicken-and-egg deadlock in 2025-2026 via hyperscaler PPAs and the ADVANCE Act, and the 17,000-to-~5,000 submarine supplier contraction plus BlueForge's ~$950M NAVSEA award are real. Held to 3 by the screener's correct kill: this is a consulting business wearing software clothes. Critical characteristics are treated as proprietary and litigation-sensitive, so the cross-buyer reusable library — the entire software thesis — may never accrete, leaving hourly-consulting margins against Curtiss-Wright/Scientech and Paragon, who already operate as third-party dedicating entities with audit histories you would spend three years earning. Still advances because even the consulting version is a real, defensible, high-margin business against a hard supply constraint.

## 24. Neutral clearing house for the HTTP 402 crawler economy

`crawler-payment-clearing-and-metering` · **AI second-order** · 🔴 **Recall-only** — no live crowding search; competitor claims are model recall

> Metering and settlement for the sub-CDN-scale long tail of sites that can currently only block or allow AI crawlers, on the supply side of an HTTP 402 market whose demand side is already forming.

**Score 4.8/10** — overlooked 2 · inflection 7 · buildable 7 · market 5 · defensibility 3

**Why now**

15 September 2026 is a hard, announced default-block enforcement date with Patreon as anchor partner, and Cloudflare is already evolving pay-per-crawl toward a pay-per-answer model that pays publishers when content appears in an AI result — instantly creating a measurement and attribution dispute the gatekeeper cannot referee for itself. Supply-side pressure is extreme and measured: small publishers lost roughly 60% of search referral traffic over two years, mid-size 47%, large 22%. The enforcement primitives shipped in parallel — Anubis matured into a sponsored project with policy tooling, honeypots and nginx/Caddy/Apache/HAProxy/WordPress integrations, and samesame wired Web Bot Auth into Anubis in November 2025 — so cryptographic agent identity is already in the wall and only settlement is missing. Neutral clearing houses historically appear 2-4 years after a payment rail reaches volume, putting the window at 2026-2028. CAVEAT: the merged 'machine as payer' candidate's claims about x402, RSL and Cloudflare launch timing were self-flagged by its scout as unverified model knowledge and should be re-checked before acting.

**Why it is overlooked**

All attention sits on the gatekeeper and on the big bilateral deals (Condé Nast, AP, Reddit, Stack Overflow, Patreon), a structure that serves the largest few hundred publishers and completely excludes the long tail that will start emitting 402s with no billing, reconciliation or dispute capability. RSL and x402 exist as specs with almost no operational tooling around them; the ecosystem below CDN scale has built blocking and nothing else, while metering and monetization are offered only by the largest edge providers — precisely the tier these operators are not on. Founders are additionally repelled by 'micropayments never work', a belief backed by twenty years of corpses (Flattr, Google Contributor, and most instructively Blendle abandoning pay-per-article); the usual diagnosis blames card interchange, but the real killer was the human decision cost per article, and the payer is now a machine with no decision friction at all.

**The 90-day wedge**

Two engineers, both halves small. Ship (a) an open-source Anubis policy plugin plus Caddy module that verifies Web Bot Auth signatures against published key directories, applies per-agent rate and price policy, and emits signed usage records — distributed free into the existing Anubis install base — with hosted monthly settlement behind it, and (b) an agent-side SDK with budget policy, per-domain caps and an exportable ledger. In 90 days: 200 long-tail publishers on the supply side (trade publications, recipe and how-to sites, specialist databases, regional publishers — the ones who will never sign a bilateral lab deal), aggregated into a single catalog under one set of terms because the buy side wants one integration not ten thousand, plus 3 AI/agent companies on the buy side. Then publish the first independent reconciliation report comparing what publishers were told they were owed against what your ledger shows. Charge basis points on settled access; the aggregation and the neutrality, not the metering, are the defensible assets.

**Why this rank**

The dated trigger is unusually concrete for an AI-native idea — over a billion HTTP 402 responses per day already, and from 15 September 2026 AI companies that do not separate search from training and agent crawlers get blocked by default across a large share of the web — and the sub-CDN long tail genuinely has no metering option. Ranked 24 because two-sided liquidity is the hardest thing to build and both sides are already claimed: the RSL Collective holds the publisher coalition, Cloudflare sits physically in the request path and can meter for free, and Skyfire/x402 own the agent wallet. Neutrality is a talking point, not a moat. The pool is also thin where you would play — per-crawl micropayments to recipe blogs are pennies while the real money concentrates in bilateral lab deals this product explicitly does not serve.

## 25. Killing lead-line 'unknowns' before the Nov 1, 2027 LCRI baseline locks in

`lead-service-line-inventory-2027` · **Regulatory clock** · 🔴 **Recall-only** — no live crowding search; competitor claims are model recall

> Drive the 'unknown' count toward zero before the LCRI baseline inventory locks on 1 November 2027, since unknowns are treated as lead and the baseline fixes each system's mandatory replacement rate for years.

**Score 4.36/10** — overlooked 2 · inflection 6 · buildable 6 · market 5 · defensibility 3

**Why now**

1 November 2027 is the statutory compliance date for the baseline inventory, the lowered 10 ppb action level, updated sampling plans and the school/childcare list — and the practical deadline is earlier, because unknowns must be resolved by field verification, which takes dig seasons. After that date most systems must replace covered lines within ten years, with all lead service lines replaced by 1 November 2037 and remaining unknowns validated by 2034, so the annual reporting, notification and progress-tracking obligation runs continuously for a decade. Systems serving 3,300 or fewer people plus all NTNCWSs get an alternative route that still requires proving 100% replacement of lead and galvanized-requiring-replacement lines at a minimum annual rate within five years — a documentation obligation, not relief. The scale of remaining ambiguity is measurable: Wisconsin Watch reported in June 2026 that more than 181,000 Wisconsin service lines, about 12% statewide, are still classified unknown.

**Why it is overlooked**

120Water, BlueConduit and Trinnex took the top of the market — municipalities with GIS staff, consultants and capital budgets. Below that, the very small community systems and non-transient non-community systems (schools, mobile home parks, small manufacturers on their own wells) are numerically the majority and commercially invisible: no procurement process, no conference presence, ACVs too small for an enterprise sales motion, and currently running on paper and Excel toward default non-compliance. At the mid-market, the specific procurement insight — that the composition of the 2027 baseline determines a decade of obligations, so verification spend in 2026-27 has a computable ROI — is being executed by regional engineering firms billing hourly; nobody has packaged it as outcome-priced work. (Evidence caveat: both scouts hit a hard egress block and could only work at search-extract level, so dates and figures below are strong leads, not page-verified citations.)

**The 90-day wedge**

Two people plus one subcontracted hydro-excavation crew, 90 days, one state. Build the predictive unknowns model from public data (parcel age, permit records, prior dig results), and sell a fixed-price 'unknowns reduction' engagement to five mid-size utilities priced as a share of avoided replacement obligation. In parallel ship the paperwork product that funds distribution: spreadsheet-import-first, takes whatever the operator has, structures it to the EPA inventory template, generates the public inventory page and annual replacement-plan document, and sends the individually-required customer notifications with delivery evidence — priced self-serve. Distribution for the small-system tier is state primacy agencies and rural water associations, which are already fielding these questions with no tool to point operators toward.

**Why this rank**

Included at 25 on the strength of one genuinely sharp mechanical insight: unknowns are deemed lead, and the 1 Nov 2027 baseline locks in a mandatory replacement rate, so every unknown resolved before that date removes capital obligation permanently — a rare case where diagnostic work has a directly computable dollar value to the buyer. The LCRI compliance date also sat under active litigation and EPA reconsideration, so even the date is not firm.

## On the shape of this list

The final 25 is concentrated, and the concentration is a finding about the research process rather than about the world. Regulatory-clock sits exactly at the permitted cap of 8 and would have been 14-16 if I had categorized by trigger rather than by the nature of the business — biosolids, copper-retirement, datacenter-grid and DPP all run off dated statutes and I classified them by what the operator actually does. Six categories carry 2+ entries (regulatory-clock 8, physical-industrial 5, ai-second-order 4, infrastructure-standards 3, graveyard-revival 2, boring-vertical 2), clearing the diversity floor, with global-markets at 1. Three categories are absent entirely: demographic, consumer-culture, and research-commercial. This is not padding avoidance — it is that every candidate in those lanes died. All four demographic entries (small-business-wind-down-desk, standby-fiduciary-for-kinless-elders, medicaid-doula-billing-infrastructure, contractor-license-qualifier-succession) were refuted by both adversarial lenses or cut on crowding, and the two strongest (wind-down desk, digital court records) had the best-verified triggers in the entire pool and still failed on unit economics and incumbency respectively. Consumer-culture (analog-media, post-pill, congregation-space, prediction-market-harm, auracast) was uniformly the weakest material in the set: no dated trigger, saturated supply, or both. Research-commercial produced two entries, muography and azole-resistance, one refuted by a funded competitor with a two-year head start and the other with no buyer at all. Two structural caveats about the underlying research. First, most of the round-2 screening and nearly all adversarial verification ran with WebSearch exhausted and WebFetch proxy-blocked, so a large fraction of both the crowding checks AND the refutations are recall to May 2026 rather than live evidence. That cuts both ways — it is why I overrode the both-refuted rule on grid-equipment-slot-forward-exchange, cui-determination-for-machine-shops and rec-state-packaging-epr, where the refuters explicitly disclosed zero live searches, and equally why I accepted the refutations of einvoice-credential-least-privilege-broker and invoice-xml-ingestion-firewall, which were built on primary artifacts (npm type definitions, GitHub REST search) rather than memory. Second, the pool has a hard quality cliff: ranks 1-13 are defensible bets, 14-17 are real but capped by market size or a specific unresolved technical risk, and 18-25 are included to reach the required count and would not clear a funding bar as written. If the mandate were 'return only what you would fund,' the honest answer is roughly thirteen names, not twenty-five.
