## THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE BUSINESS

> "The Interconnecting Transmission Service Provider (Interconnecting TSP) must submit the response to the Request for Information (RFI) to ERCOT **within 10 Business Days of ERCOT's issuance of the RFI**. The Interconnecting TSP may request a **5-Business Day extension** by emailing ERCOT at LargeLoadVerification@ercot.com."
> — Load Type E Question Reference Guide, 22 Sep 2026

And the consequence of missing it:

> "The ILLE's failure to provide necessary information and supporting documentation in time for the Interconnecting TSP to timely submit a completed response to this RFI **may result in the Large Load's removal from the Batch Zero Process**."
> — Load Type E Question Reference Guide, 22 Sep 2026

**This is not a December deadline. It is a rolling 10-business-day fire drill, fired per project at a time ERCOT chooses.**

That single fact resolves the objection that killed the original thesis. The stress test said *"the client hires the person instead"* — a procurement manager at $180–220k loaded. **You cannot staff a function that fires unpredictably and demands a complete, exhibit-indexed evidentiary package inside ten business days.** Burst work with an existential penalty and no warning is exactly what firms retain outside specialists for. The retainer is no longer the weak line in the model; it is the product.

## Claim-by-claim

### ✅ CONFIRMED — the 18-month long-lead definition

> "the ILLE has attested to the DSP or TSP that it **has ordered all equipment with a lead time of at least 18 months** and will take delivery sufficiently in advance of its requested Initial Energization date so the equipment can be installed by the ILLE's requested Initial Energization date"
> — **Planning Guide § 9.2.1.1(1)(e)(iii)**

The governing citation is **Planning Guide Section 9.2.1.1(1)(e)(iii)**. Use it; it is the hook the whole product hangs on.

### ✅ CONFIRMED, and far more specific than assumed — the evidence required

The research claimed "long-lead equipment attestations, purchase orders and delivery schedules." The actual RFI is a structured questionnaire, **LLE-1 through LLE-8**, per equipment item:

| ID | Requirement (verbatim or near) |
|---|---|
| LLE-1 | "Does the planned Load Facility require any equipment for the customer-side interconnection facilities with an 18-month lead time?" |
| LLE-2 | If No — state the basis, describe the method used to determine lead times, produce supporting documents, **plus the current one-line diagram, substation site plan and elevation drawings** |
| LLE-3 | If Yes — explain how equipment was identified and the method used to determine each item's lead time, with supporting documents "identified by document file name and page number" |
| LLE-4 | "List equipment required for the customer interconnection facilities with a lead time of 18 months or more, **including but not limited to breakers, switches, and transformers**. Assign each item a sequential number beginning with 1." |
| LLE-5 | Produce one-line diagram, site plan and elevation drawings **labelled with each item's LLE-4 number**, showing its location; "Identify any item in LLE-4 that does not appear on these documents." |
| LLE-6 | A repeating set **per item**: 6a item number · 6b item · 6c ordered Y/N · 6d order date · 6e expected delivery date · 6f **"documents showing the order (purchase orders, order confirmations, delivery schedules, or similar)"** and showing the equipment will be used at this facility · 6g affiliate/agent identification · 6h will delivery be early enough to install · 6i if No, explain why · 6j if not ordered, explain why |
| LLE-7–8 | Basis of reliance on an **Eligibility Affiliate** or a non-eligibility-affiliated third party acting as the ILLE's agent (Texas Business Organizations Code § 1.02 definitions) |

Two things the plan did not anticipate and the template must now carry: **drawings labelled per equipment item**, and **per-item explanations for anything unordered or arriving late** (LLE-6i/6j) — i.e. ERCOT explicitly invites you to explain a failure rather than hide it.

### ✅ CONFIRMED — conditional classification and removal risk

> "conditionally classified as base load under Planning Guide Section 9.2.1.1(1)(c) or 9.2.1.1(1)(d)" · "the Verification Process required before ERCOT provides final classifications of Large Loads included in Batch Zero" · "The RFI responses are used to verify that a Large Load is eligible for its conditional classification"

Removal language quoted above. A conditional classification is not an entitlement.

### ⚠️ CORRECTED — the attestation deadlines

| | Research said | Primary source |
|---|---|---|
| ILLE → TSP/DSP | 15 July 2026 *(or 24 July — sources conflicted)* | **10 July 2026** |
| TSP → ERCOT | — | **24 July 2026** |

**"15 July 2026" appears nowhere in any document read.** It was an artifact of secondary reporting. Both real dates have passed, with no extension shown.

The Timelines deck also closes a door the plan assumed might be open:

> "the post–July 10 validation period is intended to address administrative corrections, clarification requests, and minor submission errors ... **It is not intended to allow submission of materially missing eligibility items** or satisfaction of requirements that were not completed by the applicable July 10 deadline"

So the live work is genuinely **verification-phase response**, not late filing. The plan's timing correction was right in direction and is now right in detail.

### ⚠️ CORRECTED — submission routing

Routing is **mixed**, not simply "via the connecting utility":

- ILLE attestations → **Interconnecting DSP or TSP** by 10 July 2026
- TSP forms → **ERCOT**, `BatchZero@ercot.com`, by 24 July 2026
- Verification RFI → ILLE completes its portion **in RIOO**; the **Interconnecting TSP submits** to ERCOT

And a trap worth selling against:

> "Any information submitted **directly** to ERCOT pursuant to this RFI by the ILLE **may not qualify as Protected Information and therefore could be subject to public disclosure**" (ERCOT Protocols § 1.3.1.1(1)(r))

A developer who emails ERCOT its purchase orders directly may forfeit confidentiality over its equipment pricing and OEM terms. That is a concrete, checkable reason to use a desk that knows the channel.

### ⚠️ CORRECTED — the December dates

- **17 December 2026** is a **Large Load Working Group (LLWG) meeting** on ERCOT's own calendar — **not** a PUCT open meeting. No PUCT meeting on that date was verified.
- **10 December 2026** — **not found** in any primary document.
- Next LLWG meeting: **19 November 2026**.

The plan leaned on these as the forcing event. The real forcing event is the per-project 10-business-day RFI clock, which is better: it is continuous rather than a single date.

### ⚠️ CORRECTED — the eligibility threshold

> "Entities wishing to interconnect a load facility of **75 MW or greater** through the Batch Zero process should consult PGRR145 to determine their eligibility path."

**75 MW**, per the ERCOT Large Load Integration page. The 50 MW/100 kV figures in the research belong to NERC's Computational Load Entity registration, a different regime — the plan conflated two thresholds.

### ❌ NOT VERIFIED — queue scale

474 GW, 90.2% data centres, ~735 projects, 373 projects / 302.2 GW excluded: **none confirmed from primary sources.** The PUC filing that would hold them returned HTTP 402. Treat all queue figures as search-level.

### ➕ NEW — NOGRR282 is live and is a second product

> NOGRR282, "Board Priority — Large Computational Load Ride-Through Requirements." Status **"Approved on 07/09/2026"**, effective **"08/01/2026"**. "establishes frequency and voltage ride-through requirements for Large Computational Loads (LCLs)."

Already in force since 1 August 2026. This is a standing engineering-compliance obligation on large computational loads — adjacent to our buyer, on the same relationship, and it connects directly to the rank-1 opportunity in the original study (NERC ride-through modelling). Worth scoping as a second service line.

## What this does to the plan

1. **Retainer-first, not attestation-first.** The 10-business-day clock makes standing readiness the product. Lead with the retainer; the fixed-fee package becomes the readiness build that precedes the RFI.
2. **Sell to the TSP as a channel.** The TSP carries the submission obligation across many ILLEs. One TSP relationship touches dozens of projects — a far better motion than cold-emailing developers one at a time.
3. **Rewrite the artifact to the RFI's own schema.** LLE-1 → LLE-8, per item, with labelled drawings and exhibit lists. Matching ERCOT's numbering is cheap and makes the deliverable obviously fit for purpose.
4. **Cite PG § 9.2.1.1(1)(e)(iii) and PGRR145** in all outbound. It proves we have read the rule.
5. **Stop citing the December dates as deadlines.** 17 December is an LLWG meeting; 10 December is unverified.
6. **Fix the threshold to 75 MW** everywhere, and stop conflating it with NERC's 50 MW/100 kV.
